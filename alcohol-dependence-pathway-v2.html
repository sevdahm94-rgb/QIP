Acamprosate
Body-weight up to 60 kg: 666 mg once daily, to be taken at breakfast and 333 mg twice daily, to be taken at midday and at night.

Naltrexone
Adjunct to prevent relapse in formerly alcohol-dependent patients (initiated under specialist supervision): 25 mg once daily on the first day, then increased if tolerated to 50 mg daily.
Disulfiram
Adjunct in the treatment of alcohol dependence (under expert supervision): 200 mg daily, increased if necessary up to 500 mg daily.

First-line Options (NICE 1.3.6.1)
Acamprosate — initiated by IP pharmacist in the community
Goal: Maintain abstinence
Adult 18–65 years (body-weight up to 60 kg)
666 mg once daily, to be taken at breakfast and 333 mg twice daily, to be taken at midday and at night.
Adult 18–65 years (body-weight 60 kg and above)
666 mg 3 times a day.

Acamprosate: If using acamprosate, start treatment as soon as possible after assisted withdrawal.

Naltrexone: Offered as adjunct therapy in combination with an individual psychological intervention (cognitive behavioural therapies, behavioural therapies or social network and environment-based therapies) focused specifically on alcohol misuse.

Disulfiram is only indicated after a successful withdrawal for people with moderate and severe alcohol dependence. Consider offering disulfiram in combination with a psychological intervention to service users who:

  • Have a goal of abstinence but for whom acamprosate and oral naltrexone are not suitable, OR
  • Prefer disulfiram and understand the relative risks of taking the drug.

It will be reserved for selected, highly motivated patients and delivered via pharmacist-led supervised administration, aligning with NICE guidance that supervision should occur where possible.

📌 For ALL patients receiving pharmacological treatment:

Psychological support (NICE 1.3.6.1–1.3.6.2)

  • Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
  • Behavioural therapies
  • Social network & environment-based therapies
  • Behavioural couples therapy (if applicable)

Ongoing monitoring

  • Monthly supervision for minimum 6 months
  • Adherence review at each contact
  • Reassess if drinking persists at 4–6 weeks
  • Safety-net for relapse or deterioration
[1.3.6.13]Do NOT use antidepressants (including SSRIs) routinely for the treatment of alcohol misuse alone.
[1.3.6.14]Do NOT use gammahydroxybutyrate (GHB) for the treatment of alcohol misuse.
[1.3.6.15]Benzodiazepines: for withdrawal management only — NOT ongoing treatment for alcohol dependence.
